# Visual Element Catalogue — Trulo Anatomy Repository
**Purpose:** Master list of every visual element needed to build Trulo's diabetes/metabolic health content library.
**Style target:** Semi-flat illustration (Style A) for standalone assets; Infographic-Flat Hybrid (Style B) for educational compositions. Kurzgesagt-inspired biological feel — warm, elegant, not clinical.
**Brand palette:** `#D4891A` Saffron · `#8B2500` Terracotta · `#F5E6C8` Linen · `#FFD580` Golden Yellow · `#2C1A0E` Warm Brown

---

## CATEGORY 1 — ORGANS
*Primary building blocks. Each organ needs: isolated asset (PNG, transparent bg) + glowing "active state" variant*

### 1.1 Pancreas
**Why critical:** Central to the entire glucose/insulin story. The pancreas is the hero organ.
**Visual requirement:**
- Shape: Elongated, slightly curved — looks like a flattened comma or leaf. 12–15cm in the body.
- Color signature: Warm terracotta/salmon base; cream highlights; warm brown outline
- Details to show: Head, body, tail regions; hint of islets of Langerhans (small clusters that produce insulin — can be shown as tiny glowing dots)
- Active state: Slight warm glow, insulin particles streaming outward
- Fatigued state: Dimmer, slightly desaturated — for "repeated spikes damage the pancreas" narrative

**Reference style:** Similar to how Kurzgesagt shows organs — friendly rounded form, not anatomically hyper-precise
**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-flat illustration of a human pancreas, warm terracotta and saffron color palette,
soft inner highlight, minimal warm brown outline, transparent background,
clean anatomical form, visible islets of Langerhans as tiny glowing clusters,
friendly and approachable health education style, isolated organ asset
```

---

### 1.2 Liver
**Why critical:** Key glucose storage organ (glycogen); shows effects of chronic sugar overload (fatty liver)
**Visual requirement:**
- Shape: Large, lobular, dark reddish-brown. Largest internal organ — prominent, weight-bearing shape.
- Color signature: Deep warm burgundy/mahogany; terracotta highlights; golden brown outline
- Details to show: Right lobe larger than left; hint of bile ducts; portal vein entry point
- Active state: Rich warm glow — metabolically active
- Stressed state: Slightly yellowish tinge, less glow — "fatty liver" narrative

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-flat illustration of a human liver, deep warm mahogany and terracotta tones,
soft organic highlight on upper lobe, warm brown minimal outline,
prominent lobular form, health education style, isolated transparent background asset
```

### 1.3 Stomach
**Why critical:** First stop after eating; shows digestion beginning; breakdown of carbs starts here
**Visual requirement:**
- Shape: J-shaped pouch; muscular walls; visible rugae (inner folds) subtly shown
- Color signature: Warm pink-terracotta; linen interior highlights; warm brown outline
- Active state: Churning motion implied by texture; digestive enzymes visible as tiny particles
- Interior view needed: Cross-section showing food dissolving — for the "stomach chamber" scene

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-flat illustration of a human stomach in cross-section, warm pink and terracotta tones,
digestive fluid shown as subtle swirling texture, clean anatomical form,
glowing interior, health education style, isolated transparent background
```

### 1.4 Small Intestine

**Visual requirement:**
- Shape: Coiled tube; 6–7m long but shown compact; visible villi on inner surface
- Color signature: Warm beige/cream; pink highlights on villi; saffron border
- Key detail: Villi (finger-like projections) must be visible — these are where glucose absorption happens
- Absorption scene: Glucose particles passing through intestinal wall into blood vessel underneath

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-flat illustration of small intestine cross-section showing villi absorbing glucose,
warm cream and saffron tones, tiny glowing glucose particles passing through intestinal wall,
health education style, clean anatomical form, transparent background
```

### 1.5 Large Intestine (Colon)

**Visual requirement:**
- Shape: Wide frame around small intestine; segmented haustra pouches visible
- Color signature: Warm brown; muted terracotta; less vibrant than small intestine

## CATEGORY 2 — MOLECULES
*Smallest visual elements. Each molecule needs: particle form (for flow animations) + structural form (for close-up)*

### 2.1 Glucose Molecule
**Why critical:** The protagonist molecule of the entire narrative.
**Visual requirement:**
- Particle form: Small glowing sphere/hexagon; warm golden-amber color; soft glow halo
- Structural form: Hexagonal ring structure (C6H12O6); can be semi-stylized not chemistry-textbook
- Color: Warm amber/golden — should feel like energy, like sunlight
- Motion: Floats, drifts, surges — never static
- States: Normal flow (calm glow), spike (many particles, brighter), depleted (fewer, dimmer)

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-stylized glucose molecule visualization, warm golden amber glowing hexagonal form,
soft volumetric glow halo, floating in biological environment,
health education style, not chemical diagram, warm orange background implied
```

---

### 2.2 Insulin Molecule
**Why critical:** The hero response molecule. Released by pancreas to manage glucose.
**Visual requirement:**
- Particle form: Slightly different shape from glucose — more complex; perhaps a warm green-gold color to distinguish
- Structural form: Y-shaped protein hint (not precise biochemistry); elegant, purposeful
- Motion: Moves with intent; seeks and "locks onto" cell receptors
- Active state: Bright warm glow; streams out of pancreas
- Depleted state: Fewer, slower, dimmer

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-stylized insulin molecule visualization, warm gold-green glowing Y-form protein,
soft halo, purposeful floating motion, health education biology style,
distinguishable from glucose molecule
```

---

### 2.3 Fructose Molecule
**Why relevant:** Processed differently from glucose (liver only); Trulo's allulose comparison
**Visual requirement:**
- Color: Warm orange-red; slightly different shape from glucose (5-membered ring structure)
- Key scene: Fructose going directly to liver vs. glucose to bloodstream

---

### 2.4 Allulose Molecule
**Why critical:** Trulo's hero ingredient. Must look inviting, safe, almost magical.
**Visual requirement:**
- Color: White-gold crystalline; sparkly; very clean and bright
- Key scene: Allulose passing through digestive system mostly unchanged (unlike regular sugar)
- Contrast scene: Regular sugar creates spike → allulose doesn't

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Crystalline allulose sweetener molecule visualization, bright white-gold sparkle,
semi-stylized, health-safe appearance, floating cleanly through digestive system,
contrasts visually with darker sugar/glucose molecules
```

---

### 2.5 Digestive Enzymes
**Why supporting:** Break down food in stomach/small intestine
**Visual requirement:**
- Form: Irregular blob/key-hole shapes; warm amber; shown attacking food particles
- Function shown: Snipping carbohydrate chains into individual glucose units

---

### 2.6 Glucagon Molecule
**Why supporting:** Opposite of insulin; raises blood sugar when too low
**Visual requirement:**
- Color: Cool blue-green; distinct from warm glucose/insulin palette
- Scene: Pancreas releasing glucagon when blood sugar too low

---

### 2.6b Sunfiber / PHGG Gel ⭐ Trulo Product Element
**Why critical:** Sunfiber (Partially Hydrolyzed Guar Gum) is the fiber ingredient in Trulo products. Visualizing how it works in the gut is a core product claim.
**Visual requirement:**
- Form: Viscous mesh / net forming inside the small intestine
- Color: Warm translucent amber-beige gel
- Key scene: Fiber dissolving in gut water → expanding into a slow-moving gel matrix → glucose particles hitting the gel and slowing down (fewer, slower particles crossing into bloodstream vs. without fiber)
- Before/After: Without fiber = fast glucose flood. With Sunfiber = controlled trickle.

**Generation prompt seed:**
```
Semi-flat biological visualization of dietary fiber dissolving into a viscous gel
inside the small intestine, warm amber translucent mesh texture, glucose particles
shown slowing down and being filtered through the gel, educational health illustration,
warm saffron and cream color palette, not clinical, not cartoonish
```

---

### 2.6c Allulose Metabolic Pathway ⭐ Trulo Product Element
**Why critical:** Allulose's unique metabolic route is Trulo's core product story — it gets absorbed into blood but is NOT converted to glucose and is excreted in urine.
**Visual requirement:**
- Route diagram: Food → stomach → small intestine → absorbed into blood → liver detects allulose → passes through unchanged → kidneys → excreted
- Contrast with regular sugar path: Sugar → glucose spike vs Allulose → flat curve
- Key moment: Allulose and fructose look almost identical structurally (C-3 epimer) — show the structural similarity then the different metabolic outcome

---

### 2.7 GLP-1 (Gut Hormone)
**Why supporting:** Stimulates insulin release; basis for Ozempic/semaglutide; increasingly relevant
**Visual requirement:**
- Form: Elongated chain molecule; warm rose-gold color
- Scene: Released from gut cells in response to food; signals pancreas

---

### 2.8 Cortisol / Stress Hormones (Supporting)
**Why supporting:** Stress raises blood sugar — important for wholistic metabolic health narrative
**Visual requirement:**
- Color: Tense orange-red; jagged rather than smooth

---

### 2.9 Fiber Molecules (Dietary Fiber)
**Why critical:** Fiber slows glucose absorption — key to the Trulo value proposition
**Visual requirement:**
- Form: Long chain/mesh; warm brown/beige
- Function shown: Creating a "net" or "barrier" that slows glucose particle movement

## CATEGORY 2B — CELLULAR ELEMENTS
*The zoom level between molecules and organs. Essential for mechanism-of-action explanations.*

### 2B.1 Pancreatic Beta Cell (Insulin Factory)
**Why critical:** The actual cell that makes and releases insulin. Showing it gives the pancreas story depth.
**Visual requirement:**
- Form: Round/oval cell with dense insulin-containing vesicles (small spheres packed inside)
- Active state: Vesicles moving toward membrane, fusing, releasing insulin granules outward
- Color: Warm rose-pink cell body; golden amber insulin vesicles; dark background context
- Key scene: "The insulin factory inside your pancreas" — beta cell producing and secreting insulin

### 2B.2 Islet of Langerhans
**Why supporting:** The community of hormone cells inside the pancreas — beta cells (insulin) + alpha cells (glucagon)
**Visual requirement:**
- A cluster of ~10–20 cells in the pancreas tissue — like a small island in a sea of exocrine tissue
- Beta cells (warm rose) and alpha cells (cool blue-green) visible as distinct populations

### 2B.3 Insulin-Resistant Cell (Receptor "Deaf")
**Why critical:** Shows why insulin resistance happens. The lock doesn't respond even when the key arrives.
**Visual requirement:**
- Cell membrane visible; receptor present but non-responsive
- Insulin molecules arriving and "bouncing off" or unable to activate the receptor
- Glucose particles building up outside the cell unable to enter
- Interior: dim, low energy vs. healthy cell

### 2B.4 GLUT4 Transporter (The Glucose Door)
**Why supporting:** When insulin binds, GLUT4 proteins move to the cell surface and open as glucose channels.
**Visual requirement:**
- Show GLUT4 as a "door" in the cell membrane — closed (no insulin) vs. open (insulin arrived)
- Glucose particles entering through the open GLUT4 door
- Used for the lock-and-key mechanism animation

### 2B.5 Gut Epithelial Cell with Villi
**Why supporting:** The absorption interface where glucose crosses from gut into blood
**Visual requirement:**
- Finger-like villi projections clearly visible
- Glucose particles crossing through the cell from gut side into bloodstream beneath
- Sunfiber gel shown slowing this crossing

### 2B.6 Liver Hepatocyte
**Why supporting:** Processes fructose and allulose; stores/releases glycogen; "sugar accounting department"
**Visual requirement:**
- Large round cell with distinct nucleus
- Show: fructose coming in → metabolized → fat production (negative) vs allulose coming in → passes through (positive)

### 2B.7 Fat Cell / Adipocyte
**Why supporting:** Excess glucose → stored as fat; visceral fat → inflammatory signals → insulin resistance
**Visual requirement:**
- Large round cell; most of interior is a fat droplet (lipid vacuole)
- Healthy: moderate size, warm amber
- Overloaded: enlarged, yellowish; cytokine particles (small spiky shapes) being released outward

**Overlay info:** "Refined carbs eaten" marker; "Insulin released" marker; "Energy crash" marker

**Visual:** Two curves side by side; healthy (gentle rise and fall, saffron) vs. spike (sharp peak and crash, red-orange)

### 5.3 GI Scale Visual
**Visual:** Horizontal bar from 0–100; color gradient from green to red; key food markers placed
**Items marked:** Glucose=100, white rice=72, white bread=75, allulose=0, dal=25, whole wheat=50

### 5.4 Insulin Response Mechanism
**Visual:** Pancreas → insulin particles → cell receptor → glucose enters cell → energy produced
**Flow:** Arrow-driven infographic showing the complete pathway
**Healthy vs. Resistant variants:** Show clear contrast

### 5.5 "What Happens in 2 Hours After Eating" Timeline
**Visual:** Horizontal timeline; key biological events labeled at each time point
**Events:** Food eaten (0 min) → Blood glucose rises (20 min) → Insulin peaks (40 min) → Glucose normalizes (90 min) → Energy stable (2h)

### 5.6 Insulin Resistance Progression
**Visual:** 3-stage diagram — Healthy → Pre-diabetic → Type 2 Diabetic
**Organs shown:** Pancreas size, beta cells, insulin output declining across stages
**Use:** Education about how repeated spikes lead to T2D

### 5.7 Fiber's Mechanism
**Visual:** Fiber mesh/net in small intestine slowing glucose absorption
**Before/after:** Without fiber (steep spike) vs. with fiber (gentle rise)

### 5.8b Sunfiber Gel Mechanism
**Visual:** Gut cross-section showing fiber dissolving → viscous gel forming → glucose particles slowing
**Before:** No fiber = steep glucose spike curve
**After:** Sunfiber present = gentle glucose rise, delayed peak
**Use:** Core Trulo product education

### 5.8c Fructose vs. Allulose Comparison
**Visual:** Side-by-side metabolic pathway showing both molecules
**Fructose path:** → liver → converted to fat/triglycerides → stored
**Allulose path:** → liver → cannot be metabolized → excreted in urine
**Key insight:** Same structural family, completely different outcomes
**Use:** Product differentiation content

### 5.8 Allulose vs. Sugar Metabolic Path
**Visual:** Side-by-side body diagrams showing different metabolic routes
**Sugar path:** Sugar → glucose → blood spike → insulin demand
**Allulose path:** Allulose → absorbed differently → passes through → minimal insulin demand

## CATEGORY 6 — CONDITIONS (Healthy vs. Disease States)
*Essential for showing why metabolic health matters*

### 6.1 Healthy Pancreas vs. Fatigued Pancreas
- Healthy: Full, warm glow, ample insulin particles
- Overworked: Smaller, dimmer, fewer insulin particles
- Damaged: Significantly reduced beta cells visible

### 6.2 Healthy Blood Vessel vs. Inflamed/Damaged
- Healthy: Smooth inner walls; clean flow
- Inflamed: Roughened walls; AGEs (advanced glycation end products) deposits visible as dark buildup
- Atherosclerotic: Visible plaque buildup (but stylized — not raw gore)

### 6.3 Healthy Liver vs. Fatty Liver
- Healthy: Deep warm mahogany, vibrant
- Fatty: Yellowish tinge, enlarged, dull

### 6.4 Healthy Cell vs. Insulin-Resistant Cell
- Healthy: Open receptor; glucose enters freely; golden glow
- Resistant: Receptor blocked/locked; glucose building up outside; dim cell interior

### 6.5 Healthy Body Energy vs. Post-Crash State
- High energy: Glowing silhouette; bright internal systems; person standing/active
- Post-crash: Dim glow; slouched; brain foggy; weak muscle signal
